# Meykhana

The form this project was reaching for before it knew the name.

---

## What meykhana is

Meykhana (Azerbaijani: *meyxana*) is a traditional Azerbaijani practice of competitive, improvised rhymed poetry. It lives in tea houses (*çayxana*) and old pubs. Two or more poets trade couplets in real time on a given topic. The rhyme and the rhythm must be kept. Hesitation ends the turn. Wit, satire, and clear reasoning travel inside the same line.

It is not the more formal *aşıq* tradition with saz. Meykhana is the pure verbal duel: finger snaps or table taps for rhythm, immediate reply, no safety net. The whole session is temporary. When the night ends, the words are gone. Only the memory of the exchange remains.

This is specifically Azeri — especially Absheron and Baku working-class neighbourhoods. It is niche even inside Azerbaijan.

## Why it belongs here

Look at the current design of Ephemeral Burst Cache:

- short hard-TTL session
- multiple voices trading rhyme that is carried between speakers
- the rhyme is the group’s, not each voice’s private property
- an external interruption that ends the session
- only one lasting image survives

That structure was already meykhana-shaped before the name appeared. The three poets, the carried rhyme, the adult who opens the door, the disposable conversation, the single surviving photograph — all of it maps directly onto the tea-house duel.

The cultural temperature is what was missing: the pressure of the room, the expectation that reason and rhyme are one act, and the knowledge that nothing said is meant to be kept.

## Timeline of the recognition

- Project created: 15 August 2026
- Core design (three poets + photo) landed early 16 August
- The word *meykhana* and its full cultural weight arrived a few hours later the same day, while Tengri music was playing

The idea did not travel from research into the code. The code already contained the form. The name simply recognised what was already living there.

## Where this can go

When the real rooms are experienced in person — the timing of the hand-off, the exact pressure of the silence between lines, the way the room reacts — the repository can be tightened further:

- stricter turn timing
- cleaner rhyme-sound hand-off
- clearer sense of when the adult should walk in
- possibly a lighter, more disposable feel in the prompts themselves

Until then the current design is already pointed correctly.
